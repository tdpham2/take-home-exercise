**Data Understanding and Design Responses**

The graph is an extraction product, so I distinguish what a passage supports from what its node labels and relations assert. It contains **3,239 extracted and 812 inferred edges**. The examples below connect specific data problems to implemented safeguards and remaining weaknesses. They are development inspections of supplied excerpts, not verification against complete original records.

| Finding and concrete evidence | Design response | Remaining limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **Provenance describes derivation, not truth.** Inferred edge `ev_5bae6c7471a558ea` links backfill decision `decision_91e6819f` to hotfix decision `decision_64577ee4`. It has no record ID; its rationale uses shared initiative and hierarchy signals. Dates and quoted-looking text inside that rationale are still inference. | Preserve the rationale as context. Inferred edges cannot cause candidate joins or directly support facts and observed outcomes. | Useful cross-record connections can remain outside episode boundaries. Inferred context can still influence the judge; it is not independently verified support. |
| **Likely duplicated identity.** KEP-4079 names “narrative accounts API” as `resource_0e193e4f`; KEP-4072 names “Narrative-accounts API” as `resource_c9ab7b88`. Their labels match after case and punctuation normalization. | Preserve both IDs and source scopes. Shared resources do not merge episodes. | This is a plausible duplicate, not confirmed identity. No general entity resolution or alias for this API is implemented, so entity-based recall can fragment. |
| **Similar names can denote different systems.** KEP-7032 proposes upgrading the development DocDB cluster (`resource_85d8e84e`); KEP-7050 proposes upgrading the staging cluster (`resource_7947dd8e`). | DocumentDB terminology metadata preserves separate IDs and explicit development/staging environments, with `identity_merge=false`. | Shared technology does not establish a shared deployment. Alias metadata is not wired into recall, and generic “DocDB”/“DocumentDB” references remain unresolved. |
| **Routine documentation differs from execution evidence.** KT-267/KT-334 prescribe “Frequency: Twice Daily (Morning and Evening)”; KT-292 describes manual QA throughout the month. They share `decision_543399bc`. Yet KT-334's outcome `outcome_404cf4eb` says runs were executed and results logged, supported only by “resolution: Done \| status: Done.” | Fact judgments distinguish documented routines and requirements from historical observations. Exact text/subject duplicates share one judgment. The closure-only outcome is deterministically unobserved/unknown. | Repeated documentation does not prove repeated execution or compliance. The outcome label overstates its excerpt. KT records are interpreted by content, not automatically assigned a source category. |
| **An extracted source can contain unreliable cross-record synthesis.** `Community_1355` calls KEP-6672 “the retesting ticket confirming the fix.” KEP-6672 instead reports “No restriction or validation present” while specifying that hashtag addition requires source support and configuration. Another Community edge labels the fix “completed and deployed” from closure/version metadata. | The judge uses the outcome passage rather than its label to assess results. The batch-1 pilot marks the closure/version outcome `outcome_afcb364e` unobserved/unknown and recognizes the bypass as harmful. | **The Community dependency actually merges the two decisions.** Grouping currently accepts extracted dependencies without a source-reliability hierarchy. The judge can reject the resulting candidate but cannot undo the merge. |
| **Retesting is not supersession.** KEP-5480 uses `SUPERSEDES_DECISION` from `decision_63cb9ccf` to `decision_7c506018`, supported by “Retest: Deleted Pruning Rule Does Not Sync Immediately on UI.” KEP-5737 repeats this pattern. | Supersession edges neither group candidates nor automatically erase previous memory. The offline baseline flags retest-based supersession as questionable. | The excerpt establishes a retest request, not replacement or resolution. Hosted indexing bypasses the offline flags and preserves the original edge; general semantic edge repair is not implemented. |

The identity scan found **one normalized-label collision group**, using node type plus case-folded labels with punctuation removed and whitespace collapsed. That is a reproducible candidate for identity review, not an estimate of all duplicated entities. The development/staging example shows why automatically merging similar names would also be unsafe.

Source prefixes alone do not establish reliability. The graph has **54 Community records and nine KT records**, but the conclusions above concern inspected examples: procedural passages should not become execution history, and a cross-record summary should not count as independent confirmation of the records it summarizes. In the Community example, the conflict is visible within the supplied graph; it does not prove which complete original record is correct.

The current design responds through preserved identity and provenance, constrained grouping, independent judgments, administrative guards and explicit unknown outcomes. **Entity resolution, source-aware grouping and calibrated source confidence remain gaps.** Hosted items use heuristic confidence, so I do not claim that Community sources receive an implemented numerical trust discount.
